# Strangers for Mary Website Copy

Approved copy, 23 October 2026

## How the site flows

The site tells one story in three movements, **we pray, we give, we gather**, and every page ends the same way: now pass it on. It speaks to every Catholic, everywhere, who loves the Blessed Virgin Mary.

| Page | What it does for the reader | Where it sends them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Home | Shows the whole movement in one scroll and makes them want in | Join, or one of the three pillars |
| Our Story | Tells why and how Strangers for Mary started, from ten text messages in 2017 | Join |
| Pray | Explains the prayer model and the ambition behind it | Join as a stranger |
| Give | Explains how we give and why it costs us something | Join, or Build With Us |
| Gather | Lets them feel what happens when strangers meet | Join |
| Build With Us | Invites them to become one of the people who bring strangers together | Volunteer form |
| Join | One form for strangers and builders, then the share | Pass it on |

**The voice throughout:** we speak to one person, as "you". Short sentences. Present tense. Mary and the Rosary are named plainly, never hidden and never dressed up. No hype words, no exclamation marks, no em dashes. We are a network, never a family: each stranger is a bead in one long Rosary. The word "pillar" never appears on the site.

**The thread:** each pillar page can stand alone, because any one of the three can be the reason someone comes. Someone may arrive for the prayer, stay for the friendship and end up giving. The Home page carries a taste of each; the pillar pages carry the whole meal.

## Our Story page

Our Story tells why and how Strangers for Mary began, from ten text messages in 2017 to a network of hundreds, and why it is coming back stronger; it is about the reason, not about the founders.

### 1. Opening

**Headline:** It started with ten text messages.

**Body:** In 2017, a few young Catholics had just finished university and were working around the city of Buea. What they saw around them would not let them go.

### 2. What we saw

**Headline:** Faith that ended at the church door.

**Body:** Young people went to Mass and took part with their whole hearts. They sang, they prayed, they received. Then they walked out of church and were pulled straight back into a life with little room for Christ: living together before marriage, and every small vice that draws a person a little further from Him each week. Not because they had stopped believing, but because nothing carried their faith from Sunday into Monday.

### 3. What we knew

**Headline:** We had seen the Rosary work.

**Body:** We had seen it in our own lives. A Rosary prayed every day had held us close to Christ when nothing else could. Mary has never kept anyone for herself: everyone who comes close to her is handed on to her Son. So we wondered what would happen if we prayed it for each other.

### 4. Ten friends

**Headline:** "Would you be interested in a random prayer?"

**Body:** We sent that message to ten friends. Each would pray the Rosary for an intention that was not their own, and a stranger would pray for theirs. They said yes. They told their friends. Before long, the ten had grown into a network of hundreds.

### 5. When it broke

**Headline:** It grew faster than we could carry it.

**Body:** The simple tools we used to pass intentions around could not keep up, and they failed. Keeping the network running took more than we had, and we wrestled with the question every builder asks in the dark: who are we to lead this?

**Pull line:** The rest of the story is better told in person. Come and hear us tell it at a gathering some day.

### 6. Why we are back

**Headline:** Because the need never went away.

**Body:** The young people we saw in 2017 are still out there, along with millions more, in every city and every language, going to Mass on Sunday and losing the fight by Monday. This time we are building it properly: Youkaris, an app made to carry intentions safely between strangers, and a core team ready to keep the chain moving.

### 7. Our name and mark

**Body:** Our mark is the letter M for Mary, drawn as two chevrons moving forward. Between them stands a small "i". That "i" is you: every stranger, held in a personal friendship with the Blessed Virgin Mary, and carried forward by her.

**Body:** Our slogan is three words. Everything we have, our faith, our graces, our daily bread, came to us as a gift from the goodness of the Father. We do not keep it.

**Slogan:** PASS IT ON.

### 8. Close

**Headline:** Our story is still being written.

**Body:** The next chapter is the one where you take your place in the network.

**Button:** Join the strangers

## Pray page

The Pray page explains how intentions move through the network, then the heart of it: a Rosary for someone who can never thank you, the way Mary carried a need at Cana.

### 1. Opening

**Label:** We pray

**Headline:** A Rosary a day for someone who can never thank you.

**Body:** Prayer is where Strangers for Mary begins, and where it always returns. Every day of the week but Sunday, beads move through the fingers of strangers, and every one of them is moving for someone else.

### 2. How the exchange works

**Headline:** Your week as a stranger.

1. **You add your intention.** Each week, before 6 pm on Sunday, you add one intention to the many already in the network. It travels with your baptism name and nothing more.
2. **We pass it on.** We hand it to a series of strangers. Each day from Monday to Saturday, a different one takes their turn to pray a full Rosary for you.
3. **You pray for six.** Every day you receive a new stranger's intention and pray a full Rosary for it. Six days, six strangers, every week.
4. **Sunday, at Mass.** No Rosary is assigned on Sunday. Instead, you are encouraged to choose one of the week's intentions and offer a Mass for it.
5. **The chain keeps moving.** While you hold another stranger's intention, someone else is holding yours with the same care.

**Pull line:** Every decade is a gift from one stranger to another.

### 3. Every decade is a little Cana

**Headline:** We carry what is not ours to carry.

**Body:** At the wedding at Cana, Mary noticed a need that was not hers. The wine had run out at someone else's feast. She did not wait to be asked. She carried it to her Son, and He answered. Every decade we pray for a stranger, we do the same: we take to Jesus a problem that was never naturally ours to carry.

**Body:** The Rosary is simple enough for a child and deep enough for a lifetime. In it we walk with Mary through the life of her Son: his birth, his suffering, his rising, his glory. Alongside it, we keep the other Marian devotions the Church has treasured for centuries.

### 4. Why they can never thank you

**Headline:** A gift with no way back to the giver.

**Body:** Every intention carries a baptism name, but nothing that identifies the person behind it. In a network with a thousand Pauls and a thousand Marys, nobody can trace a prayer back to its owner. So the stranger you pray for can never thank you, and you never pray to be thanked. That is what keeps every gift pure.

### 5. Why it builds a habit

**Headline:** A habit for every day, not a feeling for one.

**Body:** Most of us do not stop believing. We just stop practising, one busy day at a time. A stranger waiting on your Rosary changes that. It gives every day a reason to pick up the beads, and a quiet strength against everything that pulls you away from Christ between Sundays.

### 6. Our ambition

**Headline:** A chain of Rosaries around the world that never breaks.

**Body:** We dream of a day when every intention shared with us is prayed for, every day, by someone who loves Mary. When no hospital bed, exam hall, empty kitchen or broken heart goes unprayed for. When a whole generation of Catholics, in every country, learns to live their faith every day because a stranger once needed them to.

**Close line:** It starts with one Rosary. Yours.

### 7. Where it happens

**Headline:** Youkaris. A home for daily prayer.

**Body:** Youkaris is the app that holds it all together. Each morning it hands you that day's stranger, keeps your place in the week and gently brings you back when life gets loud. It does not replace anything on your phone. It gives your daily prayer a home.

### 8. Questions people ask

**How long does it take?** A Rosary takes about twenty minutes. Many people pray it on the way to work, before bed or with their family.

**What can I share as an intention?** Anything you would bring to Mary herself, kept short. Please leave out personal details and the long history of the problem, and do not name other people in full. A few honest words, offered in faith, are enough.

**When do I add my intention?** Once a week, before 6 pm on Sunday. Each stranger adds one intention per week.

**Will anyone know it is mine?** Your intention carries your baptism name, but nothing that identifies you. Because so many strangers share the same baptism name, nobody can tell whose intention they are holding.

**What if I miss a day?** Nothing bad happens. A missed day is never punished. It is flagged, so we know who might need support, and someone makes sure you get it.

### 9. Close

**Headline:** Somebody is waiting for your Rosary.

**Body:** They will never know it was you. They will never be able to thank you. They need you anyway.

**Button:** Join the strangers

**Slogan:** Now pass it on.

## Give page

The Give page shows a charity built on small acts, each one worked for and offered for someone waiting to receive it.

### 1. Opening

**Label:** We give

**Headline:** Charity, one small act at a time.

**Body:** We are built on the charity of small actions. A meal shared. A hospital bill paid. A school fee covered. An afternoon spent with someone who is alone. None of it is grand. All of it is offered for a stranger waiting to receive it.

**Pull line:** Charity that costs us something is charity that means something.

### 2. Worked for, then given

**Headline:** We give what we have worked for.

**Body:** Strangers for Mary does not pass a collection basket. Every campaign is carried by the effort of the strangers themselves: things we make, sell, organise and build together. If we give a meal, someone worked for it. If we pay a hospital bill, someone laboured for it.

**Body:** That is what makes our charity mean something. And it means anyone can take part, whatever is in their pocket, because everyone has time and hands.

### 3. How a gift travels

**Headline:** From a stranger's need to a stranger's door.

1. **A need is found.** Through the network we hear of the sick, the poor and anyone who is struggling.
2. **A campaign is set.** The need becomes a clear goal everyone can see: what it is, who it is for and what it will take.
3. **We work for it.** Strangers take part in the activities that raise what the campaign needs, each in the way they can.
4. **We carry it.** The gift is delivered in person wherever we can, with prayer and with the respect every child of God deserves.
5. **We pass it on.** We tell the story to the whole network, so every stranger knows what their small act became.

### 4. Who we give to

**Headline:** The ones Mary would notice first.

**Body:** The sick and those who care for them. Families who do not have enough. Young people who cannot afford to keep learning. Anyone carrying something too heavy to carry alone. Like Mary at Cana, we try to see the need before anyone has to ask.

**Body:** And we give as we pray: not to be thanked, but because they are her children too.

### 5. Our ambition

**Headline:** A network that can move mountains, one pair of hands at a time.

**Body:** Imagine strangers in every country, each giving an hour of work, and all of it flowing to the same hospital ward, the same village, the same family on the same day. That is what a network of prayer can become when it learns to give. We want Strangers for Mary to be the place where Catholics everywhere turn their faith into something a stranger can touch.

### 6. Close

**Headline:** Your hands are a gift too.

**Body:** Join the strangers, and when the first campaign opens, you will be among the first to hear and the first to help. Or join the team that builds our giving from the ground up.

**Buttons:** Join the strangers · Build it with us

**Slogan:** Now pass it on.

## Gather page

The Gather page shows that we meet not to become familiar but to share the communion our prayers have made us hungry for, and to come back stronger at praying every day.

### 1. Opening

**Label:** We gather

**Headline:** It is always a party for our Mother.

**Body:** For months you have prayed for strangers known to you only by a baptism name, and strangers have prayed for you. Then, twice a year, the doors open and the strangers walk in.

### 2. Not to become familiar

**Headline:** A room full of Pauls, and nobody knows which one prayed for you.

**Body:** Everyone in the network is known by their baptism name. Even in the same room, you will never know whose intentions you carried, or who carried yours. That is not why we gather. We gather to share the physical communion that a network of prayer makes us hunger for.

### 3. What happens when we gather

**Headline:** We pray. We talk. We learn. We eat.

**We pray, praise and worship.** The beads we have held alone all season are finally held side by side. We sing loud, we fall quiet before the Lord, and we remember who all of this has been for.

**We tell the truth about prayer.** We talk honestly about the weeks we struggled and the weeks we thrived: what stopped us praying, what helped us keep going, and what we will do differently.

**We go deeper into the faith.** We take time to strengthen our understanding of what the Church teaches, so the faith we pray every day is a faith we understand.

**We break bread.** Then the tables fill, and we share a meal with the strangers we trusted to care for our faith.

### 4. Why we gather

**Headline:** This is what keeps us praying.

**Body:** Every gathering is built around one practical question: what stops us from praying every day, and how do we overcome it together? We leave with honest stories, habits that work and fresh strength for the next six months of the Rosary.

**Pull line:** We arrive as strangers. We leave stronger.

### 5. How often

**Headline:** Twice a year, the strangers meet.

**Body:** Every six months, Strangers for Mary comes together in person. Some gatherings are also held for a stranger's need, when the network rallies around someone waiting to receive help. Dates and places are announced as each gathering is set, and every stranger who signs up hears first.

### 6. Something to look forward to

**Headline:** A date every stranger circles on the calendar.

**Body:** We are not starting a new church or a new parish; our home is the parish you already belong to. A gathering is a one-time occasion, a celebration our Mother hosts twice a year, that strangers can look forward to, plan for and bring their hunger to.

### 7. Close

**Headline:** Save a seat. Better still, save two.

**Body:** Join the strangers today, and bring someone with you to the first gathering.

**Button:** Join the strangers

**Slogan:** Now pass it on.

## Build With Us page

Build With Us invites readers onto a core team of no more than twenty people, one for every mystery of the Rosary, and shows how many places are left.

### 1. Opening

**Label:** Build with us

**Headline:** Someone has to bring the strangers together. Why not you?

**Body:** Every stranger who prays will one day stand on something built by someone else: an app that works, a welcome that feels warm, a message that reached them at the right moment, a prayer guide they can trust. Those things do not build themselves. They are being built right now, by people who decided to give their gifts to Mary's mission.

**Card:** 20 places · one for every mystery · 17 still open

### 2. Twenty places

**Headline:** Twenty people. One for every mystery.

**Body:** We are building a core team of no more than twenty people, one for every mystery of the Rosary. Small enough to know each other well, strong enough to carry a network that reaches across the world. When the twenty places are filled, the team is complete.

**Counter:** 17 of 20 places still open (live count from the backend)

### 3. Why now

**Headline:** The first builders shape everything that follows.

**Body:** Strangers for Mary is at its beginning. The people who join now will set the tone, write the first words, welcome the first strangers and lay the first stones. Years from now, when a stranger on the other side of the world prays their first Rosary on Youkaris, part of what holds them will be yours.

### 4. Four teams. Find yours.

**Stranger Relations.** You are the welcome. You walk with new strangers, answer their questions, and reach out when someone misses a day, so nobody who joins is ever left alone.

**Outreach.** You are the voice. You tell our story on social media, through design and film, and in parishes, schools and communities, so the call reaches every Catholic who needs to hear it.

**Core and Content.** You are the heart. You write the prayer guides, reflections and teaching that strangers will read, always faithful to the Catechism of the Catholic Church.

**Technology.** You are the hands. You build Youkaris and the tools behind it, so that a prayer typed in one country lands safely in another.

### 5. What it asks of you

**Headline:** Your skill, your time and your love for Mary.

**Body:** You do not need to live in any particular place. Most of our work happens online, and the teams are coordinated on our own internal tool. We welcome everyone, but we keep high standards, so you may be invited to a short interview before you join the team. There are no fixed hours: the work is organised by project, and you make your own time. The one firm commitment is Sunday, when core team members work all day.

### 6. Close

**Headline:** Do not just join the strangers. Bring them together.

**Button:** Apply for a place

**Slogan:** Now pass it on.
